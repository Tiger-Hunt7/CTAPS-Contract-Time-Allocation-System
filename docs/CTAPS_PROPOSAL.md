# CTAPS Proposal — Portfolio Summary

## Contract Time Allocation and Priority System

CTAPS was proposed as a structured framework for prioritizing contract-monitoring effort across a municipal contract portfolio.

The business problem was straightforward: contract staff have finite monitoring capacity, while contracts vary substantially in operational importance, financial exposure, public impact, safety implications, compliance concerns, and vendor performance. A uniform monitoring approach therefore does not allocate staff attention efficiently.

## Proposed objective

The proposal sought to create an explainable prioritization system that would:

- rank contracts using visible and defensible factors;
- allocate monitoring attention according to relative risk and importance;
- preserve human judgment rather than replace it;
- flag uncertain or incomplete information for review;
- support a repeatable contract-monitoring workflow; and
- eventually connect the prioritization process with the organization's OpenGov and Asana workflows.

## Proposed operating model

The proposal separated the workflow into two principal analytical stages followed by a final ranking:

### Lite Ranking

Lite Ranking was intended as rapid preliminary triage using information already available in the contract database. It combined contract-title indicators with budget tiers and emphasized transparency and speed.

### Advanced Assessment

The Advanced Assessment introduced structured employee judgment for factors that could not be reliably inferred from the database alone. The broader proposal considered operational and governance factors such as criticality, health and safety consequences, legal or contractual exposure, vendor performance, public visibility, schedule sensitivity, renewal status, compliance concerns, funding source, and departmental accountability.

### Final ranking and monitoring allocation

The Lite and Advanced results were then combined into a final CTAPS index. The resulting monitoring level was intended to guide how frequently and intensively each contract should be reviewed.

## Design principle

The proposal's central principle was that CTAPS should function as a decision-support system rather than an automated decision-maker:

> The point is not to automate judgment. The point is to focus judgment where it matters most.

## Outcome

The proposal was not adopted in this form. Stakeholder review determined that the department's immediate requirement was substantially simpler than the proposed architecture. The prototype is preserved as a portfolio case study because the gap between the proposed system and the operational requirement produced an important lesson about requirements management and AI-assisted development.
